# Blind Council review 3 — Phase 5

Evaluated anonymous Responses A–E against the immutable first-draft ZIP. No named response identities or other ranking files were read. The ranking judges technical accuracy and insight, independently of the subsequent amendment audit. No customer values, live actions, source edits or design edits were used.

## Individual evaluations

**Response A.** Technically sound, with particularly useful distinctions between conservation of received units and completeness of acquisition, and between an importer-consistent validator and an independent expected population. Its remap experiment correctly follows restrictions through repair, replay and restore without rewriting event history. The bounded approval/abort envelope addresses a real availability pressure under continuing commerce without making an unsafe incumbent reactivation the fallback. Its weaker point is coverage: it misses the demonstrable false-complete KPI state, ordinary-reimport privacy suppression, inherited-restriction seed and cross-class authority race. The prior-period criticism is accurate, although the other responses more precisely identify missing attribution amounts and competing temporal clocks. A strong review; its proposed changes remain appropriately bounded.

**Response B.** Strongest overall system-safety analysis. The first draft's per-class authority ledger does not itself serialize incumbent cancellation against target booking; local OpsHub reservations cannot fence an external incumbent. The inherited restriction experiment with a pre-migration backup and zero new OpsHub markers identifies a distinct recovery coverage hole that ordinary post-dispatch tests can miss. Its completed-redaction/reimport and two-sided identity-repair critiques are also accurate, concrete and compatible with the approved architecture. Its acquisition and logical-fixture findings correctly distinguish future implementation from a missing acceptance definition. It misses the first draft's concrete KPI quality-state contradiction and gives less precise temporal/monetary examples than C or E. Some protections were already broadly intended, but B explicitly acknowledges that and asks for composition tests rather than claiming the protections absent. These limitations do not outweigh its additional consequential failure paths.

**Response C.** Very precise on remap continuity, including the danger of transferring a completed result to a different actual recipient, and on the separate old/new reservation and recovery scopes. It correctly identifies three aggregate-only monetary/prior expectations: KR16, GD31 and GD30. Its competing-clock analysis is an especially useful insight: all dates inside one window cannot detect use of the wrong cohort field, even with an exclusive-end test. Mutable acquisition is well grounded and does not assume an actual provider defect. It misses privacy on ordinary reimport, the inherited seed, cross-class incumbent conflicts and the explicit false-complete quality claim. Its findings are narrow and correct, but cover fewer of the most consequential cross-system boundaries than B or E.

**Response D.** Accurate and restrained. Privacy replay is a substantive invariant, and its golden critique correctly includes per-item attribution, prior-period members and actual competing tenant subjects. Its changing-pagination critique is also valid. It explicitly preserves the strong existing safeguards and does not demand live execution in a design phase. Its principal weakness is omission of started-effect/contact continuity during identity repair, an inherited recovery seed, cross-class authority conflicts and temporal/quality-state defects. The recommendation to complete expectations for every allocation/member-changing scenario should be read as a fixture-contract obligation before later validation passes, not a demand for provider payload implementation now; D allows an authoritative companion logical contract, which is the appropriate distinction. Sound, but offers less safety insight than the other answers.

**Response E.** Strongest concrete consistency finding: the first-draft JSON labels KR02/KR03 complete while S08 is explicitly unmapped and the approved KPI05 requires provisional quality where that uncertainty could change finality. This is a directly checkable false acceptance expectation, not only a plausible absent experiment. It also accurately describes remap suppression continuity, the danger of converting summary counts into invented events, incomplete logical inputs and changing-population capture. It appropriately allows a partial expected-value index plus a separately reviewed fixture manifest. Its omissions are the inherited seed and incompatible authority across different classes; C also supplies stronger explicit temporal and per-item money examples. E's last section is labeled as its strongest objection, but substantively reinforces its verdict rather than testing the strongest counterargument about design-phase deferral. That affects the breadth of reasoning, not the correctness of its findings. Second overall because of the concrete quality error and well-grounded safety analysis.
