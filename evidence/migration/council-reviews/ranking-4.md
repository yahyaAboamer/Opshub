Blind Stage 2 evaluation and final amendment audit — 15 October 2026

I evaluated A–E on factual accuracy, technical insight and the usefulness of their proposed proof obligations. The ranking applies to their review of the immutable first draft, not to whether their findings remain open after amendment. I read only the anonymized answers, relevant first-draft ZIP entries, the corresponding approved KPI/action/privacy/recovery contracts, and the final migration contracts and two golden JSON files. I did not read named response files, identities or other rankings. All evidence used here is documentation, safe aggregates or synthetic facts. No design edit, live inspection, migration, provider operation or recovery experiment was performed.

All five responses correctly distinguish approval of a validation design from executed proof of a safe migration. None materially misstates the approved formulas or requires reopening OD01–OD09. Their strongest findings concern missing composition of otherwise sound invariants, rather than claiming the original design had no safety controls.

**Response A**

The acquisition finding is accurate and well reasoned. First-draft MP01/PC-S02–S03/PC-B04/CT01-D require pages, fixed coverage and overlap, but do not settle how changing membership, timestamp ties or nested collections establish capture closure. The distinction between conservation of received units and discovery completeness is essential. The remap finding also correctly goes beyond retaining links: consumed contact allowance or an unknown effect must remain effective when the native subject's selected canonical association changes.

Its strongest additional insight is the independent-oracle requirement. An importer and validator can agree because both reuse the same mistaken selection. Naming shared dependencies, fixing expected populations before evaluation and expanding inspection after a false mapping are precise, bounded corrections. The approval/abort envelope under continuing traffic is another useful operational insight: stale approval invalidation alone can leave a safe but indefinitely stalled handoff.

The KR16 criticism is verified: the first draft supplies prior 15,000/four units/3-of-4 delivery without prior member identities. Its concrete test-policy criticism is also justified by policy-name placeholders. It identifies fewer of the input-specific monetary and cross-clock weaknesses than C or E, and omits the distinct cross-class, inherited-seed and ordinary-reimport privacy failures. Those omissions affect comparative insight, not the accuracy of what it does say. Its conditional-support verdict is reasonable because it explicitly makes corrections prerequisite to relying on the affected gates.

**Response B**

This supplies the strongest combined migration-safety analysis. The cross-class authority finding is specific and valid: first-draft SH/CT uses class ownership and only expressly couples replacement creation with automatic booking. The approved action architecture recognizes cancellation-versus-booking/replacement conflicts, but an OpsHub-local reservation cannot control an independently acting incumbent. Different exclusive class owners can therefore still issue incompatible actions on overlapping business scope. Requiring enforceable competing holds or grouped transfer closes that gap without inventing a lock service.

The inherited-seed finding is equally valuable. First-draft RV03/RV10 protects effects/contact starts recorded by OpsHub after backup, while imported legacy restrictions may exist without any new OpsHub marker. A restore before migration with an empty ordinary journal is a sharply discriminating test. B acknowledges that generic CT descriptor language may have intended this; it accurately identifies the missing explicit coverage obligation and experiment rather than pretending all recovery protection was absent.

Privacy suppression during ordinary re-import and two-sided quarantine after started-effect remapping are supported by the first draft's restore-focused privacy tests and unstarted-approval-focused repair language. Its changing-capture and logical-fixture findings are also accurate: the draft lacks actual MB counterparts, explicit event/result inputs and concrete clock policy rules sufficient to prevent implementation-derived expected inputs. It preserves the future authorization gates throughout. It does not identify E's direct quality-state contradiction or C's specific temporal and monetary assignments, but its unique high-consequence composition failures give it the highest overall insight.

**Response C**

The repair analysis is precise: immutable execution scope, both old/new objectives, independent descriptors and no unsupported transfer of completion are more informative than simply retaining old links. The first-draft IM-T10 only checks the repair itself for mutation; it does not prove subsequent eligibility after repair/replay/restore.

Its monetary analysis is especially concrete. KR16 lacks prior identities; GD31's 15,000 attribution lacks per-item assignment; GD30's revised aggregate does not fully specify selected and retained portion contributors. Equal aggregate totals cannot validate those assignments. C correctly distinguishes these missing expected semantics from future provider-shaped implementation.

The temporal finding adds a discriminating oracle that the other answers do not explain as specifically. Baseline qualifying dates sit in one window, and contact first-attempt/first-required-action dates coincide, allowing the wrong clock to pass. Opposite-side clocks, exclusive ends, offset ambiguity and late corrections with retained generations directly test the intended semantics. The acquisition critique is also sound. C omits ordinary-reimport privacy, inherited-only recovery and cross-class authority defects, as well as E's explicit complete-versus-provisional inconsistency. It ranks above A for its concrete independent monetary and temporal counterexamples, while A contributes useful distinct oracle and cutover-envelope insights.

**Response D**

It correctly avoids inferring a permanent universal data ban. The exact-money/prior-member and mutable-pagination findings are also supported by the first draft; the proposed companion-fixture option fits the intended design phase.

D accurately recognizes the existing baseline contributor sets and broad recovery/identity protections. I found no substantive factual error. Its comparative limitation is substantive coverage: it does not examine false-match repair after execution, inherited-only restore, incompatible cross-class owners, cross-clock equivalence or quality-state contradiction. These are important independent failure modes, not a preference for a longer answer. Its conditional approval remains logically consistent with its explicit amendment and execution conditions.
