# Independent council review — Pragmatist

Verdict: conditionally support this as the Phase 5 validation design, with the corrections below before its contracts are frozen for implementation. It is not evidence that migration or cutover is safe today. The package explicitly distinguishes specification checks from unexecuted application/provider/recovery tests, preserves unresolved policies, and keeps execution authorization separate. That is appropriate.

The core safety posture is strong: native scoped identities rather than aliases; complete source dispositions rather than forced acceptance; qualified legacy comparisons rather than reproduction of legacy errors; exact KPI contributors rather than percentage matching; one authority per action class; unknown outcomes held across fallback and restore; and privacy/current-authority gates before restored reads. I found no basis to demand a different architecture or reopen OD01–OD09. The practical weaknesses concern how some important proofs will actually be established under continuous traffic and imperfect historical identity.

Priorities below mean P1 = close the contract gap before relying on the relevant migration/action gate; P2 = strengthen the validation handoff before claiming it is executable and reviewable. These are proposed design corrections, not an implementation backlog or execution authorization.

1. **P1 — Exhausting pages does not yet establish a complete changing-source population.** Evidence: `docs/migration/03-migration-pipeline.md`, MP01 and Migration Run identity; `08-provider-contract-validation.md`, PC-S02/S03 and PC-B04; `13-cutover-and-rollback.md`, CT01-D and the paragraph after CT01-I. The design correctly acknowledges non-atomic sources and asks for overlap, fixed coverage, all pages, and buffered post-cutoff work. It does not define the acceptance proof when records enter, leave, change sort position, or change nested line collections during pagination, including a change whose timestamp equals the checkpoint. A locally complete disposition ledger can still account perfectly for an acquisition that silently omitted an object. The same problem affects discovering old open work outside the proposed history window.

   Correction: add a per-source acquisition-completeness contract. It must identify the qualified snapshot/cursor/watermark semantics, cutoff and tie handling, nested-object completeness, treatment of disappeared/deleted objects, and replay/dedup boundaries. Where the provider cannot supply a stable snapshot or bounded change contract, explicitly qualify what completeness can be proven by independently reconciled reads/exports and hold unprovable operational scope. Add an acquisition fault scenario in which objects move across pages and change at the cutoff while a page is retried. Expected evidence must include the independently known source member set and each final revision or an explicit unresolved coverage result, not merely equal row counts or two successful reads. This is a proof requirement, not a prescription for a particular provider algorithm.

2. **P1 — False-match repair needs a positive proof that action and contact holds follow the corrected business identity.** Evidence: `02-identity-mapping.md`, IM02 and IM-T10; `03-migration-pipeline.md`, Corrected mapping re-run; `13-cutover-and-rollback.md`, Corrected mapping and re-import. These passages preserve history, preview old-to-new links, invalidate unstarted approvals, and prohibit mutation during re-import. They do not explicitly test subsequent eligibility after an erroneous crosswalk is detached. If a contact budget or unknown booking was attached to the wrong Order/case, merely retaining it on that old canonical identity can leave the corrected identity appearing unused and eligible. No external mutation during the repair itself would still pass IM-T10.

   Correction: specify that repair reconciles all contact ordinals, unknown/succeeded effects, handovers, objective reservations, native-result bindings, and independent recovery descriptors affected by either identity. Preserve original recording lineage; do not silently move or duplicate factual events. Until reliable native/business scope resolves which subject owns an obligation, conservatively hold both affected scopes or the wider connection where necessary. Extend IM-T10 with a false match containing exhausted contact allowance and an unknown external effect; correct it, replay the source, restore a pre-correction backup, then attempt fresh approvals/contact starts against both subjects. The proof must show no reset allowance or newly executable duplicate objective, with safely unrelated work still distinguishable.

3. **P2 — KR16 remains a scalar comparison example despite the exact-member standard.** Evidence: `07-kpi-regression-suite.md`, KR16; `06-golden-datasets.md`, Envelope and reproducibility; `evidence/migration/golden-contracts.json`, `facts`, `products`, `isolated_deltas`, and policy declarations. KR16 states prior sales, units, and delivery totals and says denominator sets are explicit, but the prior-window identities, item amounts, and shipment sets are not supplied there or in the JSON. The current window is much better specified. Several fixture policy dimensions are also a version label rather than a concrete test rule, while contact/confirmation/pickup dates are date-only. This leaves future test authors deciding part of the expected answer, especially at cohort boundaries, rather than implementing a settled oracle.

   Correction: supply an explicit logical prior-period fixture with ordered contributor IDs, per-item amounts/quantities, final/outcome membership, exclusions, and period boundaries. Define the finite TEST-POLICY-v1 cohort/precision rules or supply explicit timestamps where required; do not approve production OP policies by doing this. Add a prior-window equal-total/wrong-member mutation and an exclusive-end boundary mutation with exact expected set changes. The JSON may remain a logical specification rather than a provider payload, but mark any still prose-only scenarios accurately and make their required expected facts unambiguous. Provider/DB fixture realization can remain in the later phase.

4. **P2 — An independent validator must not reuse the importer’s mistaken selection as its expected truth.** Evidence: `03-migration-pipeline.md`, MP10 and its independent-validation diagram; `05-legacy-regression-oracle.md`, Difference adjudication; `12-shadow-and-parallel-run.md`, representative trusted expected results; `14-reconciliation-and-signoff.md`, technical-reviewer sign-off. The design demands exact diffs and accountable explanations, but it does not state which evidence is independent of the transformation being tested. A shared parser, crosswalk, or source selector can drop or misassign the same members in both actual and expected outputs. An Admin-approved explanation can establish a current selection decision; it cannot alone validate the historical match that produced it.

   Correction: require the reconciliation report to identify how each expected set was obtained and any shared implementation dependencies. Use an independently enumerated raw-source population for conservation/acquisition proof and an evidence-reviewed sample of accepted identities and qualified/unresolved groups for semantic proof, with all critical operational holds included. Source-grounded expected results should be fixed before evaluating actual results. Explain how a false mapping discovered by review expands inspection to the affected rule/source group. This need not mean a second full migration engine or a statistical claim of perfect accuracy; it means making circular verification visible and disallowing it as the sole proof.

5. **P2 — The cutover contract needs a bounded approval-and-abort protocol under continuing commerce traffic.** Evidence: `13-cutover-and-rollback.md`, CT01-A/D/G/H and current-state drift invalidation; `14-reconciliation-and-signoff.md`, Sign-off ownership and invalidation; `16-open-migration-decisions.md`, MD11–MD13/MD15. The design appropriately allows commerce to continue, holds mutation/contact starts, and invalidates stale activation approval. As written, it is unclear which arriving source changes require a complete renewed approval versus a bounded delta validation, and how long the pilot may remain without an action authority while that review repeats. A busy merchant can remain safe but stuck, accumulating work until operators bypass the process. Existing queue-age/response targets do not explicitly settle this transition.

   Correction: make the future approved cutover envelope include an exact validated base, the kinds of post-cutoff changes allowed into buffered catch-up, the material changes that invalidate approval, a maximum held-start window tied to merchant deadlines, and the named abort/escalation decision. Rehearse continuous new-order and source-update arrivals through approval and a failed return to the incumbent. Successful abort may mean continued controlled hold when transfer is unsafe; it must not imply immediate Gentry reactivation. Report the operational backlog and accountable handling during that gap. No universal duration or new numerical SLA is needed in Phase 5; MD11–MD13/MD15 should explicitly own these parameters.

Assumptions: Phases 1–4 are approved exactly as stated by the owner, while residual OP/A/MD choices remain unresolved. This review treats current repository documents as authoritative for scope and examined all Phase 5 documents 00–17, the golden JSON, approved owner decisions, and the relevant architecture data, analytics, external-action, and recovery contracts. It did not inspect private customer rows, execute imports/tests, qualify provider capabilities, or observe production behavior. Findings describe contract gaps and plausible failures, not verified defects in an implemented system. The high-level obligations already present in the package should be retained; the corrections make their acceptance proofs more precise.

Strongest objection to my verdict: the package is intentionally a design, and MD16 plus the future assertion format explicitly defer executable fixture and toolchain realization. One could reasonably approve it now and require all five details during that realization. My response is that the acquisition, remapping, and independent-oracle gaps determine what counts as a passing implementation; leaving them entirely to the implementation author risks a green suite that proves its own assumptions. Conversely, these are bounded additions. They do not justify rejecting the substantial safety work or demanding implementation, live experiments, deployment, or cutover during this phase.
