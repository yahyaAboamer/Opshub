# Blind Council Stage 2 review and amendment audit

Reviewed 15 October 2026. This review ranks the original anonymized Responses A–E on accuracy and insight, separately from auditing the subsequently amended contracts. No named response files, lens identities or other rankings were read. The original ZIP was read selectively to check cited first-draft assertions; no original evidence or design document was edited. Only documentation, synthetic facts and safe aggregates were inspected. No application, migration, provider, recovery or production experiment was executed.

The strongest original answer is Response B, followed closely by Response E. B identifies the most consequential omitted compositions across incumbent authority, inherited restrictions, privacy and identity repair. E supplies the clearest directly demonstrable contradiction in the first-draft expected results. The amended package now addresses their substantive findings and the additional contributor, clock, acquisition and independent-oracle concerns. It is suitable for human review as a validation design, subject to its stated future gates; it does not prove execution readiness.

## Individual evaluations of the original answers

**Response A — accurate and useful, with particularly good independence and operational-continuity reasoning.** Its distinction between conserving received units and proving acquisition completeness is technically correct. Its remapping example correctly shows that preserving history does not establish that duplicate-prevention and contact restrictions still govern the corrected subject. It appropriately requires independently known member/revision expectations rather than successful repeated reads. Its independent-validator critique is valuable: sharing a mistaken source selector or crosswalk can make actual and expected outputs agree while both are wrong. Its bounded approval/abort proposal also recognizes that indefinite held starts can become an operational failure under continuing traffic.

A's weaker points are omissions at other high-risk boundaries. It does not isolate the cross-class incumbent race, restore-before-migration with no OpsHub dispatch markers, completed privacy dispositions bypassed by ordinary re-import, or the explicit complete/provisional contradiction in the first-draft golden index. Its contributor amendment concentrates on the prior period rather than also specifying the partial revenue attribution and revised spend portions. Date-only evidence is not intrinsically inadequate for an approved calendar-date cohort; its concern is strongest where instant selection or spacing is actually required. Its acquisition and remapping findings are proof gaps, not demonstrated provider or implementation defects, and it correctly acknowledges that limitation. These findings merit adoption, but C and E expose more precise weaknesses in the interpretation oracle.

**Response B — best overall technical and safety insight.** The cross-class counterexample is persuasive and distinct from a routine exclusive-owner check. One owner for booking and another owner for cancellation can each satisfy their per-class ledger while issuing incompatible effects against overlapping scope. A target system's local reservation cannot serialize an autonomous incumbent. B asks for enforceable competing holds or grouped transfer without pretending a new distributed lock service solves the problem.

Its inherited-restriction counterexample is equally strong: ordinary pre-dispatch journaling can be complete yet contain nothing about a legacy unknown booking or previously used allowance when no new OpsHub dispatch occurs. Restoring a pre-migration backup therefore requires an independently retained migration seed, not merely replay of new dispatch markers. Its privacy finding distinguishes ordinary import/replay from restore, and its remap finding protects both old and new subjects after a possibly executed effect. All four are concrete ways the named first-draft tests could pass without exercising a dangerous transition. Acquisition closure and independent logical input specification are also correct, bounded additions.

B does not catch the golden trust-state contradiction or make the temporal/monetary cases as explicit as C and E. Some broader invariants in the first draft arguably imply its protections, as B itself acknowledges; its strength is showing precisely why the existing named tests do not establish them. I rank it first for these independent, consequence-focused counterexamples, not for its number of findings or length.

**Response C — strong precision on remapping, amounts and competing clocks.** It correctly refuses both loss of a reservation and unsupported transfer of completion when a false association is repaired. Its union-of-scopes hold and immutable execution-time native intent are a sound bridge between current identity correction and independent recovery. Its contributor critique is more complete than A's: it identifies missing prior members, item-specific partial attribution and revised spend portions, all cases in which scalar equality can conceal wrong semantics.

C's strongest distinctive insight is temporal identifiability. When entry, placement, first attempt, action requirement and observation all fall in the same baseline window, selecting the wrong clock can still return the right member set. Requiring opposite-side boundary examples, a late correction and offset ambiguity makes the intended clock choice falsifiable. Its mutable-pagination argument also correctly rejects acquisition conservation as sufficient completeness proof.

C omits B's cross-class and zero-new-marker inherited restrictions, the ordinary privacy replay gap and E's explicit quality-state contradiction. Its named temporal cases are an important test-contract improvement rather than an observed deployed defect. That is accurately stated. It ranks above A because its additional amount and clock counterexamples expose specific ways an interpretation suite can give false reassurance even with otherwise exact member checks.

**Response D — sound but less complete at the important composed boundaries.** Its ordinary-intake privacy finding is correct, including the distinction between completed suppression and permitted restricted evidence holds. It avoids unsafe phone-based scope inference and does not invent a permanent reacquisition ban. Its exact monetary contributor and prior-period critique is substantiated, and it correctly offers an authoritative logical companion contract instead of requiring provider payload implementation during Phase 5. Its acquisition critique is precise about changing listings, disappearance versus deletion, nested pagination and independently known convergence.

D accurately recognizes existing protections and separates design approval from execution evidence. Its lower position reflects missing consequential findings rather than brevity: it does not test remap after a started/unknown effect, inherited restrictions through restore without new markers, incompatible incumbent classes, competing clocks or the erroneous complete quality expectation. Its three amendments are valuable but cover fewer independent failure mechanisms than the other answers.

**Response E — the strongest directly verifiable oracle contradiction, and a close second overall.** The immutable first-draft index actually labels KR02 and KR03 complete while S08 has unmapped finality. This is a concrete inconsistency between acquisition completeness and interpretation trust, not just a request for additional adversarial coverage. The correct known-subset ratios do not justify a complete quality claim when an unknown outcome could change final membership. E also correctly distinguishes genuinely mapped pending from unmapped finality rather than downgrading every pending population mechanically.

Its effect/contact continuity amendment is comprehensive, protects execution-time scope through re-import and restore, and rejects automatic reservation release or completion transfer. Its logical-fixture critique is well grounded: policy labels, aggregate counters and a scenario-ID list cannot independently settle missing raw identity/event/clock semantics. Its qualification is important, however: the logical contract need not already be a provider-shaped executable fixture if the index is accurately labeled and an independent complete realization is an explicit gate. Its acquisition closure critique is also accurate.

E does not identify ordinary privacy replay, the cross-class incumbent race or inherited legacy restrictions absent from a post-migration dispatch journal. It ranks below B because those latter irreversible-action and recovery counterexamples add more independent safety insight. Its demonstrated trust-state inconsistency and remap argument keep it above C and A.

## Residual gaps and future gates

I found no residual substantive safety-contract blocker after rereading the final corrections. The package now identifies the protected scope, failing experiment and fail-closed outcome for the accepted cross-boundary critiques. The original summary/line-ID wording discrepancies and TC02 chronology concern are corrected in the final content inspected.

Complete provider-shaped inputs and exact output envelopes for every scenario remain unfinished artifacts, explicitly required before V2 passes. Some scenario variations and the mapped-pending contrast remain prose contracts. This is a bounded future realization gate, not proof that all golden cases are already reproducible or executed, and not a concealed claim of design completeness at the provider-payload level. The review/independence gate must remain binding so a future author cannot fill missing evidence from implementation defaults.

Actual provider closure semantics, old-history access, event continuity, account ownership, incumbent stop enforceability, negative-result evidence, recovery-storage enumeration/protocol compatibility and measured restore behavior remain unqualified. MD01–MD16 and their OP/A dependencies leave source authority, production clocks/eligibility, operational deadlines, privacy holds/retention and signers to accountable decisions. These are future execution/policy prerequisites with explicit held outcomes; their unresolved status is not an instruction to build or experiment during Phase 5.

Human review can approve the amended validation design with those gates intact. Nothing in this audit authorizes migration, infrastructure changes, production shadow, provider writes, customer contact or cutover, and no documentation or arithmetic pass should be reported as proving a safe pilot today.
