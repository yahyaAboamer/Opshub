## Individual evaluations

**Response A:** Accurate and strong on the distinction between complete acquisition and conservation of whatever was received. Its union-of-identity reservation concern correctly reaches beyond preserving historical associations, and the proposed restore experiment tests a dangerous composed failure. Its two distinctive insights are independence of expected truth and a bounded approval/abort envelope under continuing traffic. Both matter: a green validator can share the importer's omission, and a safe cutover can become operationally untenable without a named held-window owner. It respects the existing architecture and future authorization boundaries. It misses the explicit complete/provisional contradiction and the specific cross-class and inherited-restriction recovery failures. The population-closure finding's P1 label is defensible for operational continuity, although the draft's general coverage holds already mitigate it.

**Response B:** The strongest overall safety reasoning. It identifies that one owner per class is insufficient when booking and cancellation/replacement remain independently executable in different systems. It also distinguishes ordinary post-dispatch recovery markers from inherited legacy restrictions: restore-before-migration with zero new OpsHub markers is a particularly discriminating experiment. Privacy replay, two-sided remap quarantine, mutable acquisition and independent raw fixture inputs complete a coherent set of boundary failures. The failure paths and proposed corrections are precise without selecting an implementation. Its assertion that broad invariants may already imply parts of these rules is appropriately qualified. It does not catch the concrete metric trust-state inconsistency or supply C's detailed clock-discrimination cases, but its distinct action/recovery insights have greater overall safety consequence.

**Response C:** Accurate, technically careful and particularly insightful about temporal oracles. It explains why generic end-boundary tests can pass with the wrong clock and proposes cases that separate occurrence, process entry, order placement, observation and correction recording. Its monetary comparison critique correctly distinguishes contributor amounts from matching totals, and its remap argument avoids silently transferring completion to the wrong subject. Acquisition closure is well framed as qualification rather than an assumed provider defect. It does not cover privacy resurrection, inherited legacy seed coverage, incompatible cross-class authorities or the complete/provisional mismatch. These omissions make its combined safety assessment less comprehensive than A, B and E; this judgment concerns substantive coverage, not response length.

**Response D:** Its three findings are accurate and bounded. Privacy suppression must survive ordinary re-import as well as restore; prior-period and monetary allocation expectations need exact members; and exhausted mutable pagination is not closed-population proof. It recognizes that a separately reviewed companion fixture contract can be sufficient and accurately preserves existing protections. It omits the more consequential composed remap/recovery and split-authority failures and the direct trust-state inconsistency. The conditional-approval conclusion is reasonable, but its described conditions leave more of the original question's critical safety paths unchallenged than the other responses.

**Response E:** The clearest directly verifiable internal contradiction: original KR02/KR03 label unmapped S08 finality `complete`, even though unknown finality can change the final denominator. Read-only inspection of the original ZIP confirmed those values. It correctly separates complete capture from complete interpretation rather than changing the approved ratio. Its remap/reservation failure and the warning against synthesizing source events from summary booleans are also strong. Acquisition closure and independently specified logical inputs are appropriately scoped to contracts. It misses B's split-authority and zero-new-marker recovery cases and A's operational approval envelope. Its assertion that the JSON is not a complete fixture is correct; it also fairly permits an explicitly partial index plus a separately reviewed complete realization gate.

## Narrow findings resolved during this audit

1. **TC02 chronology and confirmation-clock discrimination:** The earlier current supplement had order placement on 18 August and confirmation entry on 16 August while expecting trusted prior confirmation membership, without specifying an anomaly qualification. That unnecessarily mixed the intended clock test with anomalous chronology. Current TC02 now has placement 15 August, entry 16 August and observation 18 August, preserving a valid earlier-entry/later-observation test. Added TC09 has placement 16 August, entry 18 August and observation 19 August, with TCF02 in current confirmation numerator/denominator and absent from prior members. Together they discriminate both wrong-observation and wrong-placement clocks while preserving valid chronology. I verified the JSON values and KR18/GD37 references after correction. No remaining finding from this item.

2. **Repeated-line wording:** `06-golden-datasets.md` initially said the supplement contained repeated identical native line IDs, but its actual LINE-X1/LINE-X2 fixture correctly used distinct line identities for identical variant contents. The current document now says repeated identical line contents with distinct native IDs. The prose and logical expected two-item/two-unit result agree. No remaining finding from this item.

These were bounded logical-contract corrections, not evidence of an implemented software defect. The original answer ranking is unchanged by their resolution.
