# Blinded answers — Phase 5 safety review

Question: Does the first complete design safely migrate a controlled Gentry pilot and prove correct OpsHub interpretation? Evaluate technical accuracy and insight, not style or length. Answers refer to the immutable first-draft snapshot.


## Response A

Verdict: conditionally support this as the Phase 5 validation design, with the corrections below before its contracts are frozen for implementation. It is not evidence that migration or cutover is safe today. The package explicitly distinguishes specification checks from unexecuted application/provider/recovery tests, preserves unresolved policies, and keeps execution authorization separate. That is appropriate.

The core safety posture is strong: native scoped identities rather than aliases; complete source dispositions rather than forced acceptance; qualified legacy comparisons rather than reproduction of legacy errors; exact KPI contributors rather than percentage matching; one authority per action class; unknown outcomes held across fallback and restore; and privacy/current-authority gates before restored reads. I found no basis to demand a different architecture or reopen OD01–OD09. The practical weaknesses concern how some important proofs will actually be established under continuous traffic and imperfect historical identity.

Priorities below mean P1 = close the contract gap before relying on the relevant migration/action gate; P2 = strengthen the validation handoff before claiming it is executable and reviewable. These are proposed design corrections, not an implementation backlog or execution authorization.

1. **P1 — Exhausting pages does not yet establish a complete changing-source population.** Evidence: `docs/migration/03-migration-pipeline.md`, MP01 and Migration Run identity; `08-provider-contract-validation.md`, PC-S02/S03 and PC-B04; `13-cutover-and-rollback.md`, CT01-D and the paragraph after CT01-I. The design correctly acknowledges non-atomic sources and asks for overlap, fixed coverage, all pages, and buffered post-cutoff work. It does not define the acceptance proof when records enter, leave, change sort position, or change nested line collections during pagination, including a change whose timestamp equals the checkpoint. A locally complete disposition ledger can still account perfectly for an acquisition that silently omitted an object. The same problem affects discovering old open work outside the proposed history window.

   Correction: add a per-source acquisition-completeness contract. It must identify the qualified snapshot/cursor/watermark semantics, cutoff and tie handling, nested-object completeness, treatment of disappeared/deleted objects, and replay/dedup boundaries. Where the provider cannot supply a stable snapshot or bounded change contract, explicitly qualify what completeness can be proven by independently reconciled reads/exports and hold unprovable operational scope. Add an acquisition fault scenario in which objects move across pages and change at the cutoff while a page is retried. Expected evidence must include the independently known source member set and each final revision or an explicit unresolved coverage result, not merely equal row counts or two successful reads. This is a proof requirement, not a prescription for a particular provider algorithm.

2. **P1 — False-match repair needs a positive proof that action and contact holds follow the corrected business identity.** Evidence: `02-identity-mapping.md`, IM02 and IM-T10; `03-migration-pipeline.md`, Corrected mapping re-run; `13-cutover-and-rollback.md`, Corrected mapping and re-import. These passages preserve history, preview old-to-new links, invalidate unstarted approvals, and prohibit mutation during re-import. They do not explicitly test subsequent eligibility after an erroneous crosswalk is detached. If a contact budget or unknown booking was attached to the wrong Order/case, merely retaining it on that old canonical identity can leave the corrected identity appearing unused and eligible. No external mutation during the repair itself would still pass IM-T10.

   Correction: specify that repair reconciles all contact ordinals, unknown/succeeded effects, handovers, objective reservations, native-result bindings, and independent recovery descriptors affected by either identity. Preserve original recording lineage; do not silently move or duplicate factual events. Until reliable native/business scope resolves which subject owns an obligation, conservatively hold both affected scopes or the wider connection where necessary. Extend IM-T10 with a false match containing exhausted contact allowance and an unknown external effect; correct it, replay the source, restore a pre-correction backup, then attempt fresh approvals/contact starts against both subjects. The proof must show no reset allowance or newly executable duplicate objective, with safely unrelated work still distinguishable.

3. **P2 — KR16 remains a scalar comparison example despite the exact-member standard.** Evidence: `07-kpi-regression-suite.md`, KR16; `06-golden-datasets.md`, Envelope and reproducibility; `evidence/migration/golden-contracts.json`, `facts`, `products`, `isolated_deltas`, and policy declarations. KR16 states prior sales, units, and delivery totals and says denominator sets are explicit, but the prior-window identities, item amounts, and shipment sets are not supplied there or in the JSON. The current window is much better specified. Several fixture policy dimensions are also a version label rather than a concrete test rule, while contact/confirmation/pickup dates are date-only. This leaves future test authors deciding part of the expected answer, especially at cohort boundaries, rather than implementing a settled oracle.

   Correction: supply an explicit logical prior-period fixture with ordered contributor IDs, per-item amounts/quantities, final/outcome membership, exclusions, and period boundaries. Define the finite TEST-POLICY-v1 cohort/precision rules or supply explicit timestamps where required; do not approve production OP policies by doing this. Add a prior-window equal-total/wrong-member mutation and an exclusive-end boundary mutation with exact expected set changes. The JSON may remain a logical specification rather than a provider payload, but mark any still prose-only scenarios accurately and make their required expected facts unambiguous. Provider/DB fixture realization can remain in the later phase.

4. **P2 — An independent validator must not reuse the importer’s mistaken selection as its expected truth.** Evidence: `03-migration-pipeline.md`, MP10 and its independent-validation diagram; `05-legacy-regression-oracle.md`, Difference adjudication; `12-shadow-and-parallel-run.md`, representative trusted expected results; `14-reconciliation-and-signoff.md`, technical-reviewer sign-off. The design demands exact diffs and accountable explanations, but it does not state which evidence is independent of the transformation being tested. A shared parser, crosswalk, or source selector can drop or misassign the same members in both actual and expected outputs. An Admin-approved explanation can establish a current selection decision; it cannot alone validate the historical match that produced it.

   Correction: require the reconciliation report to identify how each expected set was obtained and any shared implementation dependencies. Use an independently enumerated raw-source population for conservation/acquisition proof and an evidence-reviewed sample of accepted identities and qualified/unresolved groups for semantic proof, with all critical operational holds included. Source-grounded expected results should be fixed before evaluating actual results. Explain how a false mapping discovered by review expands inspection to the affected rule/source group. This need not mean a second full migration engine or a statistical claim of perfect accuracy; it means making circular verification visible and disallowing it as the sole proof.

5. **P2 — The cutover contract needs a bounded approval-and-abort protocol under continuing commerce traffic.** Evidence: `13-cutover-and-rollback.md`, CT01-A/D/G/H and current-state drift invalidation; `14-reconciliation-and-signoff.md`, Sign-off ownership and invalidation; `16-open-migration-decisions.md`, MD11–MD13/MD15. The design appropriately allows commerce to continue, holds mutation/contact starts, and invalidates stale activation approval. As written, it is unclear which arriving source changes require a complete renewed approval versus a bounded delta validation, and how long the pilot may remain without an action authority while that review repeats. A busy merchant can remain safe but stuck, accumulating work until operators bypass the process. Existing queue-age/response targets do not explicitly settle this transition.

   Correction: make the future approved cutover envelope include an exact validated base, the kinds of post-cutoff changes allowed into buffered catch-up, the material changes that invalidate approval, a maximum held-start window tied to merchant deadlines, and the named abort/escalation decision. Rehearse continuous new-order and source-update arrivals through approval and a failed return to the incumbent. Successful abort may mean continued controlled hold when transfer is unsafe; it must not imply immediate Gentry reactivation. Report the operational backlog and accountable handling during that gap. No universal duration or new numerical SLA is needed in Phase 5; MD11–MD13/MD15 should explicitly own these parameters.

Assumptions: Phases 1–4 are approved exactly as stated by the owner, while residual OP/A/MD choices remain unresolved. This review treats current repository documents as authoritative for scope and examined all Phase 5 documents 00–17, the golden JSON, approved owner decisions, and the relevant architecture data, analytics, external-action, and recovery contracts. It did not inspect private customer rows, execute imports/tests, qualify provider capabilities, or observe production behavior. Findings describe contract gaps and plausible failures, not verified defects in an implemented system. The high-level obligations already present in the package should be retained; the corrections make their acceptance proofs more precise.

Strongest objection to my verdict: the package is intentionally a design, and MD16 plus the future assertion format explicitly defer executable fixture and toolchain realization. One could reasonably approve it now and require all five details during that realization. My response is that the acquisition, remapping, and independent-oracle gaps determine what counts as a passing implementation; leaving them entirely to the implementation author risks a green suite that proves its own assumptions. Conversely, these are bounded additions. They do not justify rejecting the substantial safety work or demanding implementation, live experiments, deployment, or cutover during this phase.

## Prioritized findings

### 1. P1 — Exclusive authority per action class does not exclude incompatible actions across classes

**Evidence:** `docs/migration/12-shadow-and-parallel-run.md`, “Controlled action pilot ordering” and “Parallel run authority”; `13-cutover-and-rollback.md`, CT01-B/H. The ledger switches one class at a time. The explicit coupling example is replacement creation triggering native booking. Architecture `06-external-action-safety.md`, “Execution protocol and concurrent Admins,” recognizes cancellation-versus-booking/replacement conflicts, but its local reservations cannot serialize an incumbent action.

**Failure:** OpsHub owns booking while Gentry/manual processes still own cancellation or replacement. Each class has exactly one authority, yet both systems can act on the same order using stale evidence. The signed per-class ledger remains correct while an incompatible pair of effects occurs. A later discrepancy cannot undo cancellation or physical dispatch.

**Correction:** Add a cross-class conflict/dependency matrix to the authority ledger. Transfer conflicting classes together, or explicitly hold the incumbent conflicting scope until the target operation is settled. Require a CT/EA test racing incumbent cancellation against OpsHub booking, and incumbent replacement against OpsHub cancellation. State how the shared prohibition is enforced when Gentry cannot consume OpsHub reservations; if enforcement is unavailable, the conflicting class stays held. This need not introduce a shared distributed lock service.

### 2. P1 — Inherited legacy holds and contact usage need an explicit independent recovery seed

**Evidence:** `03-migration-pipeline.md`, “Inbound does not replay history”; `11-recovery-validation.md`, RV03/RV10 and RV-G3; `13-cutover-and-rollback.md`, CT01-B/E/F. Architecture `13-deployment-and-environments.md` writes independent records before OpsHub dispatch, handover or contact issuance. The migration design preserves imported unknown effects and historical allowances, but does not explicitly require independently surviving records for every inherited restriction before enabling actions.

**Failure:** A legacy unknown booking or used contact allowance is imported, no new OpsHub effect/contact is issued, and a later restore predates the migration. The ordinary OpsHub dispatch journal can have complete coverage while containing no record of that inherited restriction. Reacquired provider data may not reconstruct an uncertain legacy call or missing-native-reference effect. A new case/objective then appears eligible.

**Correction:** Define a signed, minimal migration safety manifest covering inherited unknown effects, handovers, consumed/uncertain contact allowances, unsupported live episodes and their native/business scopes. Independently retain it before authority transfer, and include its enumeration/coverage in RV-G3. Add a restore-before-migration experiment with **zero post-migration OpsHub dispatch markers**. Missing seed coverage must quarantine the affected scope. CT01's generic descriptor requirement may intend this already; make that obligation and its test explicit.

### 3. P1 — Completed privacy dispositions must constrain future imports, not only restores

**Evidence:** `15-privacy-and-test-data.md`, “Cleanup and retention decisions” and PV07–PV09; `03-migration-pipeline.md`, replay/new-run rules and MP-T09; `13-cutover-and-rollback.md`, “Corrected mapping and re-import.” Architecture privacy/recovery contracts explicitly replay dispositions after database restore. They do not explicitly specify how an old snapshot, changed-parser run or delayed source receipt is prevented from reintroducing previously redacted data into ordinary access.

**Failure:** Redaction completes correctly across current data. A later approved re-import reads an earlier immutable capture and creates a fresh observation or generation with the removed fields. No database restore occurs, and the existing privacy-during-staging test need not exercise a disposition that predates the new run.

**Correction:** Require acquisition/intake, normalization and publication to consult the current source-scoped privacy disposition, including late arrivals and superseded files. Define authorized evidence-hold exceptions and minimized suppression identifiers under MD14; do not assume all source data may be retained or all must be deleted. Add tests for same-snapshot replay, a changed importer/mapping version, late callback, and old-generation export after a completed disposition. Expected results must separately cover restricted intake, canonical fields, projections and downloads.

### 4. P1 — Identity repair after execution starts needs a two-sided effect quarantine

**Evidence:** `02-identity-mapping.md`, IM02/IM-T10; `13-cutover-and-rollback.md`, “Corrected mapping and re-import.” Both preserve old associations and invalidate **unstarted** dependent approvals. EA14 tests edited intent, but there is no named experiment for correcting a false native/canonical association after an effect may have been sent.

**Failure:** An incorrectly crosswalked shipment/action is attached to order A; its effect is unknown or successful. A reviewed repair moves the selected association to B. A now appears to lack a shipment or completed objective, while its original dispatch scope still has consequences. Preserving historical links alone does not specify which identities remain nonactionable, and a newly derived objective can bypass the old reservation.

**Correction:** State that identity repair never rekeys or releases a started/unknown effect's original reservation. Quarantine both affected subjects and linked objectives until independent native/business-scope reconciliation establishes which actions remain permissible. Carry contact allowances conservatively through disputed case remaps. Add an IM/EA/CT experiment: false match → accepted/lost-response effect → remap → new request on each side. Assert zero additional mutation/contact starts until the affected scopes are resolved, with both histories retained.

### 5. P2 — “All pages plus overlap” is not yet a proof of a closed capture boundary

**Evidence:** `03-migration-pipeline.md`, MP01 and MRUN01; `08-provider-contract-validation.md`, PC-S02/S03 and PC-B04; `13-cutover-and-rollback.md`, CT01-D. The package correctly rejects a fictitious globally atomic snapshot and requires fixed coverage, overlap and post-cutoff buffered work. It does not specify the acceptance proof when records move during pagination, updates share a cutoff timestamp, or a previously captured child/object disappears.

**Failure:** All requested pages succeed, every captured unit has a disposition, and counts reconcile. An object moved across the pagination boundary was never captured, or a removed order line remains selected indefinitely. Conservation of captured rows cannot detect either problem. A configurable overlap is not a completeness guarantee without a qualified delay/order bound or an independent reconciliation process.

**Correction:** Require provider-specific capture-closure evidence: ordering/cursor and boundary semantics, inclusive overlap/dedup behavior, buffering/reconciliation across initial-to-incremental handoff, and explicit withdrawal/deletion treatment that distinguishes missing data from authoritative removal. Add mutations between page reads, equal-boundary updates, late receipt outside the initial overlap, and removed child-item scenarios. Where the provider cannot establish a bound, require repeated inventory reconciliation and an explicit incomplete/held scope rather than a complete flag. Preserve historical assertions while correcting current membership.

### 6. P2 — Golden contracts do not yet fix enough input semantics to prevent circular validation

**Evidence:** `06-golden-datasets.md`, “Envelope and reproducibility” and GD27/GD28/GD34; `07-kpi-regression-suite.md`, “Result assertion envelope”; `evidence/migration/golden-contracts.json`, `merchants`, `facts`, `policies`, `required_scenarios`. The JSON declares MB and reused aliases, but supplies no MB business objects that collide with MA. Several fact groups lack explicit tenant/account, cohort clocks or reporting-window inputs. Contact facts mostly provide aggregate attempts/reached/action booleans. `TEST-POLICY-v1` names policies without defining the relevant clock/selection rules in the JSON. Many scenarios are listed rather than supplied as input/expected-output pairs.

**Failure:** Future fixture realization can fill those gaps from the implementation's own defaults, directly seed already-interpreted booleans, and compare resulting contributor sets to the same assumptions. The KPI arithmetic passes while raw-to-canonical identity, event continuity, period selection or tenant binding is wrong. Exact member comparisons are necessary, but only test the members represented by independently specified inputs.

**Correction:** Keep the current JSON explicitly a partial semantic expected-value catalog. Before treating the golden contract as complete, specify a minimal independent source-to-canonical fixture layer: actual MA/MB collisions, distinct repeated item IDs, contact events versus summaries, declared clocks/precision, source-account bindings, reporting parameters and expected dispositions/bindings. Define test-policy rules and intentional omissions without selecting production OP policies. No importer implementation is requested. Require future realization to preserve that approved input/expected mapping and prove that deliberately wrong parsers/mappers fail.

## Assumptions and limits

## Findings, in priority order

### 1. P1 — False-match repair needs an explicit contact/effect reservation continuity contract

**Evidence:** `02-identity-mapping.md`, “Collision and replay rules,” IM02 and IM-T10; `03-migration-pipeline.md`, “Failure, rollback and re-import”; `13-cutover-and-rollback.md`, “Corrected mapping and re-import.” These require retained old links/history, recomputation and invalidation of **unstarted** dependent approvals. The migration test catalog does not compose these rules when the identity correction itself changes which canonical Order/case owns the native subject.

**Failure to test:** A false crosswalk attaches a native subject to synthetic Order OA; an actual or uncertain call/booking consumes or reserves its scope. A reviewed repair detaches that association and binds the native subject to OB. Re-import creates OB's apparently unused case/objective, or a later restore reconstructs only the old OA hold. Keeping the historic record and invalidating future approvals does not, by itself, prove that OB cannot obtain another allowance or executable objective. Conversely, blindly moving the completed result can falsely certify work on a different actual recipient/subject.

**Correction:** Add an explicit remap barrier and adversarial IM/EA/RV contract covering started, succeeded, unknown and manual-handover effects plus consumed/uncertain contact allowances. Preserve immutable execution-time native scope and intent; identify every affected old/new objective; block affected starts while continuity is ambiguous; reconcile current reservations and independent recovery descriptors without rewriting history. Test repair, replay and restore after repair. Expected result: no new allowance or duplicate effect merely because a canonical association changed, and no transfer of completion without evidence. ### 2. P2 — Some claimed contributor-level golden results still contain only scalar expectations

**Evidence:** `07-kpi-regression-suite.md`, “Result assertion envelope,” requires exact member IDs and amounts. Its KR16 row states prior sales 15,000, units 4 and delivery 3/4, but supplies no prior contributor identities or amounts. `06-golden-datasets.md` GD31 and JSON `partial_attribution` specify attributed 15,000/unattributed 5,000, but do not define which item amounts received that attribution. JSON `spend_revision` similarly specifies aggregate revised allocation rather than a full revised contributor result. The baseline has much stronger item and shipment sets.

**Failure to test:** Wrong prior shipments can yield the required 3/4, and two different item attribution assignments can both yield 15,000 and ROAS 3.0. A test author must invent the missing expected assignments or derive them from implementation output, weakening the independent oracle exactly where the suite rejects scalar-only equality.

**Correction:** Specify synthetic prior-period Orders/items/shipments, amount/basis per contributor, exact exclusion sets and complete current/prior comparison envelopes. For GD31, name each item's attributed/unattributed amounts and test-only eligibility basis. For GD30, spell out selected revision/portion contributors and retained prior results. These may remain logical JSON/document contracts; no executable importer or production policy decision is needed. Verify a same-total/wrong-item or wrong-prior-shipment mutation fails.

### 3. P2 — The temporal oracle does not distinguish the competing clocks it says must remain separate

**Evidence:** `06-golden-datasets.md`, “Envelope and reproducibility,” places baseline qualified dates on 18–20 August within a single window. JSON contact facts give first-attempt and first-action dates on the same date for all cases that have both. Confirmation and pickup entry dates and shipment creation also sit inside that window. `07-kpi-regression-suite.md`, “Cohorts,” requires exclusive-end, undated and timezone checks, but gives no concrete cross-clock expected sets. The architecture deliberately distinguishes occurred/effective/source-updated/observed/ingested/calculated clocks.

**Failure to test:** Using observation date for confirmation entry, order date for shipment-created cohorts, or first-attempt date for action-required cohorts can pass the present baseline. A generic exclusive-end test can also pass while evaluating the wrong field. This matters to shadow comparison because later-arriving evidence can silently rebucket historical work.

**Correction:** Add small synthetic cases where relevant clocks lie on opposite sides of a window boundary, including action required before the first attempt, shipment creation after order placement, late observation of earlier occurrence, and later correction with an earlier effective date. Declare exact members for each KPI and old/new generation. Include a named timezone-offset transition and an ambiguous local timestamp with the expected hold/qualification. State the test-only clock selection concretely rather than relying only on the `TEST-POLICY-v1` label. Production clock decisions remain MD06.

### 4. P2 — Pagination completeness needs an adversarial changing-population acceptance test

**Evidence:** `03-migration-pipeline.md` MP01 demands proof of all pages/coverage; `08-provider-contract-validation.md` PC-S02/S03 and PC-B04 require complete reads and overlapping incremental recovery; `13-cutover-and-rollback.md` CT01-D records bounded final reads, source skew and buffered work. CT-T03 covers changes during approval, but no named contract demonstrates discovery completeness when the source population changes during pagination itself.

**Failure to test:** A traversal can exhaust every returned cursor while an object moves across the already-read boundary, a late-visible update retains an earlier source timestamp, or a nested line connection changes between pages. Every acquired object can have a disposition and every checkpoint can be consistent while a required object was never acquired. Row conservation alone cannot detect this omission.

**Correction:** Extend provider/migration contracts with insert, update, delete/unavailability and nested-child changes between controlled page barriers. Require a provider-qualified snapshot/high-water/overlap/reconciliation method, declared limitations and exact expected identity/revision sets. When the provider cannot support a completeness claim, preserve explicit coverage uncertainty and block continuity-dependent activation rather than equating exhausted pagination with complete discovery. This does not assume any particular provider has the defect; it ensures the future qualification can detect it.
