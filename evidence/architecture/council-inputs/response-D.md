Recommend conditional approval of the architectural direction, with the two recovery contracts below tightened before Phase 4 is considered complete. It is faithful to OD01–OD09 and plausibly implementable by a small team. The essential invariants justify a single transactional PostgreSQL system, explicit domain grains, separate merchant/platform authority, and durable external-effect records. The strongest parts are the refusal to equate queue delivery with external execution, the hold on unknown outcomes across connected/manual paths, and analytics that preserve overlapping product populations and conserved monetary allocations. Those are necessary correctness mechanisms.

This assessment assumes a modest pilot with the documented single-account bindings, a team comfortable with TypeScript and PostgreSQL, approved source/analytics policies, and qualified provider result matching. No reviewed document proves those operational assumptions. Chapters 17–18 correctly keep account capabilities, competing booking actors, attribution, and service plans as release qualifications. Integrated operations and Product/SKU/ROAS remain mandatory.

1. **P1 — The recovery marker cannot necessarily rebuild a forgotten reservation.** In [13-deployment-and-environments.md](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/13-deployment-and-environments.md), “Protecting external effects across backup rollback,” the marker stores generated objective/intent/effect IDs, an intent hash, and connection/correlation references. Suppose the objective itself was created after the restored database point: its UUID and hash do not reveal the original order, action kind, or reserved business scope. Provider correlation might also be unqualified. Global dispatch blocking remains safe, but the promised reconstruction of missing objective reservations is underspecified. Include a versioned, minimally identifying recovery envelope containing stable subject/source references, action/effect kind, reservation scope, and relevant dependency/result identities; retain encrypted minimum intent context only where genuinely required. Add a restore fixture that loses every local action row while preserving the marker and provider effect. It must recover the correct block without authorizing a new booking.

2. **P1 — Database rollback can resurrect revoked access.** [03-multi-tenancy-and-identity.md](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/03-multi-tenancy-and-identity.md), “Authentication, sessions and invitations,” puts sessions and local authority in PostgreSQL; chapter 13 says expired membership/invitation/session policies are rechecked. A restore can lose a recent suspension, privilege reduction, invitation use, or session revocation while restoring an apparently active record. Checking expiry or that restored record cannot establish current authority. Mutation-egress-off does not protect customer reads or exports. Define recovery to invalidate restored sessions/invitations through a new recovery epoch and independently reverify merchant memberships, platform assignments, and service restrictions before reopening access. For a small pilot, explicit audited revalidation is simpler than another general security ledger. Test revocation and invitation consumption after the restore point, including read/export denial.

3. **P2 — Bound the provenance machinery to named MVP concepts.** [04-data-architecture.md](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/04-data-architecture.md), “Common record contracts,” and [11-audit-provenance-and-privacy.md](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/11-audit-provenance-and-privacy.md), “How a belief is explained,” introduce subject anchors, assertions, interpretations, corrections, domain versions, and tenant revisions. The required result is explainable selected truth with preserved disagreements; it does not require a universal interpretation engine. Maintaining generic selections and typed domain values creates a second consistency problem. Specify a finite MVP concept/field catalog and one transactional ownership rule for each selected value. Consolidate physical history records where sensible, retain evidence references, and keep business constraints in typed modules. This preserves provenance while reducing opportunities for AI-generated abstraction and duplicated state.

4. **P2 — Justify two scheduling layers for ordinary safe work.** [07-background-jobs-and-reconciliation.md](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/07-background-jobs-and-reconciliation.md), “Delivery and transactional semantics,” combines outbox dispatch/confirmation/sweeps, business-work claims, and pg-boss transport/leases. External actions need independent business truth regardless of queue choice. Routine sync/projection/export work may not need a parallel scheduler lifecycle as well. Identify which work requires module-owned state and which needs only durable transport, retry, and a domain checkpoint. Prefer one scheduling authority; retain extra state only for a concrete invariant. Queue replaceability alone is a weak MVP reason to build more coordination machinery.

The stack is a credible default: explicit SQL, ordinary HTTP, a managed identity provider, and one web/worker release. Its suitability still depends on actual team skills and purchased-plan economics; the declared compatibility gates should be checked at the start of a later authorized build.

The strongest objection to this recommendation is that the architecture already demands global blocking and independent review during recovery, so neither recovery gap necessarily permits an unsafe action. That is fair. The concern is making those stop conditions executable and recoverable, rather than leaving engineers to invent the missing contract under incident pressure. Simplification must preserve these safety obligations.
