Approve the overall architectural direction, subject to bounded recovery amendments before Phase 5. This is faithful to OD01–OD09: separate truth dimensions, stable contact budgets, Merchant Admin authority, integrated Shopify/Bosta workflows, and explainable Product/SKU/ROAS analytics. A modular monolith, PostgreSQL, one worker and relational projections are suitable for a small team. The reservations, precommitted dispatch, unknown-result holds and prohibition on automatic compensation are strong choices. Architecture approval must remain separate from pilot readiness.

My assumptions are the explicitly bounded pilot account model, reviewed owner policies, current provider qualification before enabling capabilities, and operations staff who can investigate unknown effects. Public capability documentation establishes neither account access nor recoverable execution. This review assessed documents; no application, isolation, provider or recovery tests have run.

1. **P1 — Restore can resurrect authority and deleted data.** [13, “Protecting external effects across backup rollback”](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/13-deployment-and-environments.md), with [03, “Authentication, sessions and invitations”](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/03-multi-tenancy-and-identity.md) and [11, retention/redaction](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/11-audit-provenance-and-privacy.md). Sequence: backup while a user is Admin; revoke membership and approve customer redaction; restore the backup. The live IdP still authenticates the user, and the restored local grant is active. Reauthentication alone cannot reveal the lost revocation. A deletion manifest stored only in the restored database also disappears. Rechecking expiration is insufficient. Specify an independently retained revocation/redaction record with coverage verification, or fail closed and recertify grants before reopening. Invalidate restored sessions/invitations, reconcile platform assignments and connection ownership, and replay independently preserved redactions before any user read or export. Add this exact scenario to 14.

2. **P1 — The recovery journal does not yet specify enough surviving identity to rebuild lost reservations.** [13, journal protocol](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/13-deployment-and-environments.md), [06, fallback handover](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/06-external-action-safety.md). A post-backup order/objective can be created, booked, and then lost locally. The marker lists opaque IDs and an intent hash; those do not recover the subject, effect kind, business scope or original/replacement relationship. A handover followed by manual booking can also lose its local exclusion without an OpsHub dispatch marker. Provider reconstruction may be incomplete, as 18 acknowledges. Define the minimal recoverable, privacy-reviewed subject/source/effect linkage and independently journal handover before releasing execution to a human. Where identity or journal coverage cannot be reconstructed, quarantine the entire affected connection/merchant scope; do not invent a narrow objective from an unresolvable UUID. Establish retention and enumeration coverage that cover every restorable backup. S3 conditional creation prevents overwrite, but deletion markers can permit a fresh write; the specified deletion protection must therefore be enforceable. [AWS conditional-write behavior](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonS3/latest/userguide/conditional-writes.html).

3. **P2 — Safe unknown-result handling may create an operational dead end.** [06, “Action versus effect state”](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/06-external-action-safety.md), [17 A02](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/17-open-architecture-decisions.md), [18 Bosta duplicate-booking qualification](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/18-provider-capability-review.md). Crash after committing ExecutionStarted and confirming the marker, before sending. OpsHub correctly holds the effect, but an absence search cannot establish no effect. If the provider offers neither qualified idempotency nor authoritative negative proof, the order can remain blocked indefinitely, including manual fallback. Keep this safety rule. Strengthen each enabled mutation’s qualification with a demonstrated recovery route, accountable human/provider escalation and explicitly accepted unresolved-case handling. A happy-path round trip cannot satisfy this gate. This is a feasibility risk, not permission to downgrade the approved integrated MVP.

4. **P2 — Keep the implementation surface smaller than the logical catalog.** [04, record groups](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/04-data-architecture.md), [07, jobs](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/07-background-jobs-and-reconciliation.md), [15, stack](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/15-technology-selection.md). The design combines typed domain tables, anchors, assertions, interpretation history, generations, contributors, business work and queue transport. All have reasons, but independent generic frameworks for each would overwhelm a small team and increase authorization mistakes. Specify fixed typed records and reviewed queries, reuse closely related history/subtype storage where safe, and prohibit configurable rule/event/provenance engines. Keep only the proposed managed utilities; toolchain and plan gates should resolve before their dependencies become entrenched.

The strongest objection to my recommendation is that the recovery amendments touch the most consequential promises, while provider recovery remains unproved. That could justify withholding approval entirely. I favor bounded approval because the design already fails closed and explicitly identifies those qualifications; require the amendments and evidence rather than replacing a coherent monolith with more infrastructure.
