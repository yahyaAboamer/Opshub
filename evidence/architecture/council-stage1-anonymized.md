# Architecture council — independent anonymized answers

Is the OpsHub Phase 4 architecture a safe, faithful, implementable MVP for a small AI-assisted team, preserving OD01-OD09?

## Response A

Request two focused recovery corrections before approving this architecture as implementation-ready. Retain the proposed modular monolith, shared PostgreSQL, separate worker, REST boundary and managed authentication. This is a faithful foundation for the approved MVP: separate business/physical truth, Merchant Admin authority, one outbound, linked replacements, integrated operations and Product/SKU/ROAS all have architectural homes. The treatment of unknown effects, duplicate provider results, overlapping product populations and missing inputs is unusually careful. No warehouse, microservice fleet or native financial engine is needed.

My assumptions are a modest pilot, one qualified operational source account of each relevant kind per merchant, a team comfortable reviewing TypeScript and SQL, and an identified human who owns provider incidents and restore decisions. Provider contracts in chapter 18 are documentation evidence; account access, reliable result matching and side effects remain unproved. Architecture approval cannot certify AC14/AC15, tenant security or recovery performance. OD01–OD09 should remain closed.

1. **P1 — Make the independent dispatch journal reconstructable after losing its database references.** In `13-deployment-and-environments.md`, “Protecting external effects across backup rollback,” the marker stores local objective/intent/effect/attempt IDs, a hash, authority reference and connection/correlation ID. The same section requires reconstructing missing reservations after restore. If those rows were created after the restore point, opaque IDs and a hash cannot reveal the business subject, action kind or reserved scope. A subsequently re-ingested order can acquire a different canonical identity, leaving recovery unable to attach the hold correctly. Specify a minimal independently retained recovery envelope: verified provider-account namespace, stable native subject bindings where available, operation/effect scope, objective relationships and the necessary binding/intent references. Protect any sensitive recovery subset with encryption and reviewed retention. Add the explicit design oracle in chapter 14: restore before the entire objective existed, then ingest the subject again; a second connected or manual effect must remain blocked. Global suspension is safe containment, but successful recovery still needs this mapping.

2. **P1 — Recover revocations and deletions independently of the rolled-back database.** Chapters 03 and 11 place sessions, memberships, invitations, audit and deletion manifests in persistent records; chapter 13 requires rechecking expired authority and replaying deletion manifests. A restore can resurrect a still-unexpired session, a revoked Operator/Admin/platform grant, a used invitation or customer data deleted after the checkpoint. Fresh OIDC login does not repair a restored local membership. Specify where post-checkpoint security and privacy changes survive, how completeness is established, and when user access can reopen. A practical recovery contract invalidates restored sessions/invitations, reapplies independently retained deletion/revocation records and reverifies grants when complete history is unavailable. Apply the access gate to reads/exports as well as mutation dispatch. Include post-backup suspension, invitation acceptance and redaction scenarios in chapter 14. The dispatch journal currently addresses only one class of rollback consequence.

3. **P1 release condition — Prove manageable recovery for the exact core provider actions.** Chapters 06/18 correctly avoid assuming universal Shopify idempotency or Bosta uniqueness/exhaustive lookup. Chapters 17 A01/A02 therefore carry substantial operational risk: a timeout can immobilize an essential case indefinitely while both normal execution and fallback remain prohibited. Preserve that safety rule. Make qualification acceptance explicitly cover ambiguous creates, reliable positive matching, permissible terminal-negative evidence, competing automatic booking and the human/provider escalation route. Identify the responsible Merchant Admin and support evidence they can obtain without granting platform customer access. Budget investigation time in chapter 16 and choose incident targets through A06. A successful happy-path round trip alone cannot demonstrate that the small team can operate this MVP safely.

4. **P2 — Put a firm limit on the provenance and work machinery.** Chapters 02/04/07/09/11 introduce numerous logical records, generic subject anchors, field assertions, interpretation revisions, an outbox plus queue transport, and retained analytical generations. These serve real requirements, but they can consume the team before the merchant loop works. The documents already permit physical consolidation and reject event sourcing. Strengthen that constraint: concrete module commands, bounded typed concepts, aggregate-level revisions where sufficient, one authoritative work state, and only requested/exported plus bounded recent analytical generations. Preserve evidence links, conflicts and effect identities. Avoid independently configurable framework machinery or duplicate retry histories across outbox and queue.

The strongest objection to requesting changes is that chapter 13 already blocks reopening when coverage or authority is uncertain, so these omissions need not cause an immediate unsafe write. That is valid. My concern is whether a small team can identify the affected subjects and reopen useful service without lost revocations or indefinite whole-merchant suspension. Explicit recovery evidence and access gates resolve that concern while retaining this sound architectural direction.

A modular monolith, PostgreSQL, one worker and relational projections are suitable for a small team. The reservations, precommitted dispatch, unknown-result holds and prohibition on automatic compensation are strong choices. Architecture approval must remain separate from pilot readiness.

My assumptions are the explicitly bounded pilot account model, reviewed owner policies, current provider qualification before enabling capabilities, and operations staff who can investigate unknown effects. Public capability documentation establishes neither account access nor recoverable execution. This review assessed documents; no application, isolation, provider or recovery tests have run.

1. **P1 — Restore can resurrect authority and deleted data.** [13, “Protecting external effects across backup rollback”](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/13-deployment-and-environments.md), with [03, “Authentication, sessions and invitations”](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/03-multi-tenancy-and-identity.md) and [11, retention/redaction](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/11-audit-provenance-and-privacy.md). 2. **P1 — The recovery journal does not yet specify enough surviving identity to rebuild lost reservations.** [13, journal protocol](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/13-deployment-and-environments.md), [06, fallback handover](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/06-external-action-safety.md). S3 conditional creation prevents overwrite, but deletion markers can permit a fresh write; the specified deletion protection must therefore be enforceable. 3. **P2 — Safe unknown-result handling may create an operational dead end.** [06, “Action versus effect state”](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/06-external-action-safety.md), [17 A02](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/17-open-architecture-decisions.md), [18 Bosta duplicate-booking qualification](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/18-provider-capability-review.md). Crash after committing ExecutionStarted and confirming the marker, before sending. OpsHub correctly holds the effect, but an absence search cannot establish no effect. If the provider offers neither qualified idempotency nor authoritative negative proof, the order can remain blocked indefinitely, including manual fallback. Keep this safety rule. Strengthen each enabled mutation’s qualification with a demonstrated recovery route, accountable human/provider escalation and explicitly accepted unresolved-case handling. A happy-path round trip cannot satisfy this gate. This is a feasibility risk, not permission to downgrade the approved integrated MVP.

4. **P2 — Keep the implementation surface smaller than the logical catalog.** [04, record groups](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/04-data-architecture.md), [07, jobs](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/07-background-jobs-and-reconciliation.md), [15, stack](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/15-technology-selection.md). The design combines typed domain tables, anchors, assertions, interpretation history, generations, contributors, business work and queue transport. All have reasons, but independent generic frameworks for each would overwhelm a small team and increase authorization mistakes. Specify fixed typed records and reviewed queries, reuse closely related history/subtype storage where safe, and prohibit configurable rule/event/provenance engines. Keep only the proposed managed utilities; toolchain and plan gates should resolve before their dependencies become entrenched.

The strongest objection to my recommendation is that the recovery amendments touch the most consequential promises, while provider recovery remains unproved. That could justify withholding approval entirely. I favor bounded approval because the design already fails closed and explicitly identifies those qualifications; require the amendments and evidence rather than replacing a coherent monolith with more infrastructure.

OD01–OD09 are preserved. Shopify/Bosta integration and Product/SKU/ROAS remain MVP requirements without assuming undocumented provider guarantees. The TypeScript/Nest/Kysely stack is reasonable; adding services, a warehouse or another queue datastore would weaken this proposal.

Key assumptions: A01/A02 genuinely block unqualified mutations; unknown results can require extended human/provider investigation; the pilot accepts unavailable/provisional analytics until attribution inputs qualify; and A04–A07 receive explicit policy decisions. This is architectural assessment, not evidence of tested application safety or a certified connected round trip.

1. **P1 — Restore can resurrect revoked authority and deleted data.** `13-deployment-and-environments.md`, “Backup and restore” and “Protecting external effects across backup rollback,” require rechecking authority and reapplying deletion manifests. However, the only explicitly independent journal covers provider effects. Membership revocation, platform assignment removal, session invalidation, service suspension and deletion manifests can themselves disappear during database rollback. Checking restored rows cannot discover those lost changes. A previously revoked Admin/session could become active again; a lost erasure manifest cannot redact resurrected records. Remedy: define the independently retained recovery authority for revocations/redactions and its coverage, invalidate restored sessions/invitations, and require verified grant/service-state reconciliation before user access or mutation release. Preserve a fail-closed recovery state when that evidence is incomplete. Add this specific rollback scenario to chapter 14; expiration checks alone are insufficient.

2. **P1 — Surviving dispatch markers may not reconstruct business reservations.** `13`, “Protecting external effects across backup rollback,” lists objective/intent/effect/attempt IDs, hash, authority reference and connection/correlation ID. Consider an objective and connection created after the restore point: their marker survives while their database rows disappear. Those UUIDs and an intent hash do not identify the missing order/native namespace or recover the objective’s reservation scope. The instruction to “rebuild missing objective reservations” therefore lacks a sufficient recovery contract. Remedy: specify versioned, minimal noncustomer recovery fields identifying stable provider account, business subject and operation/reservation scope, plus deterministic association to rediscovered source objects. Define complete journal enumeration/coverage and the precise tenant/source quarantine when reconstruction is impossible. A marker remains “may have sent”; additional recovery fields must never manufacture approval or success.

3. **P1 — Contact concurrency is controlled after the physical call.** `04-data-architecture.md`, “Transactions and storage layout,” locks the objective when appending contact; `08`, “Concurrency matrix,” allocates the ordinal then. The authoritative WF03 calls externally before recording. Two Operators can see the same due case, both call, and only subsequently contend on recording. Database ordinals cannot undo the extra call or restore the 24-hour spacing. Remedy: make existing claim/contact capabilities an atomic pre-call eligibility and attempt reservation with one responsible Operator. Specify abandoned/uncertain reservation handling conservatively; lease expiry must not silently restore allowance if a call might have occurred. Separate authorization/budget usage from evidence capture, so an evidenced unauthorized or excess call remains recordable as a violation. This requires no telephony integration and preserves OD04.

4. **P2 — Case identity must anchor call KPI denominators.** `04`, “Common record contracts,” makes return/pickup subtypes unique and contact objectives stable, but leaves the cardinality between `operational_cases` and `contact_objectives` unspecified. `09`, “Operational KPI calculations,” counts distinct cases. Multiple confirmation/contact cases sharing one objective could preserve the three-attempt budget while multiplying reach/action-completion denominators; attaching reach to one duplicate changes the rate. Remedy: define the canonical case-to-objective identity/cardinality and uniqueness contract, reuse that case on reopening, and make contributors follow it. Ambiguous imported case associations remain excluded/diagnostic rather than generating additional cases. Include duplicate-case/reopen contributor fixtures in chapter 14.

The strongest objection to this recommendation is that architecture documents legitimately defer physical schemas and detailed runbooks. Chapters 13/17 already demand fail-closed restoration and pilot qualification, so these gaps need not produce unsafe writes. That is persuasive for conditional approval of the topology and stack. It is insufficient for unconditional implementability: the missing information and linearization points determine whether recovery can ever safely resume and whether OD04/KPI guarantees mean what the product promises. Correct those contracts without expanding MVP scope.

This assessment assumes a modest pilot with the documented single-account bindings, a team comfortable with TypeScript and PostgreSQL, approved source/analytics policies, and qualified provider result matching. No reviewed document proves those operational assumptions. Chapters 17–18 correctly keep account capabilities, competing booking actors, attribution, and service plans as release qualifications. 1. **P1 — The recovery marker cannot necessarily rebuild a forgotten reservation.** In [13-deployment-and-environments.md](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/13-deployment-and-environments.md), “Protecting external effects across backup rollback,” the marker stores generated objective/intent/effect IDs, an intent hash, and connection/correlation references. Include a versioned, minimally identifying recovery envelope containing stable subject/source references, action/effect kind, reservation scope, and relevant dependency/result identities; retain encrypted minimum intent context only where genuinely required. 2. **P1 — Database rollback can resurrect revoked access.** [03-multi-tenancy-and-identity.md](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/03-multi-tenancy-and-identity.md), “Authentication, sessions and invitations,” puts sessions and local authority in PostgreSQL; chapter 13 says expired membership/invitation/session policies are rechecked. The stack is a credible default: explicit SQL, ordinary HTTP, a managed identity provider, and one web/worker release. Its suitability still depends on actual team skills and purchased-plan economics; the declared compatibility gates should be checked at the start of a later authorized build.

The strongest objection to this recommendation is that the architecture already demands global blocking and independent review during recovery, so neither recovery gap necessarily permits an unsafe action. ## Response E

I recommend **conditional approval of the architecture**, after the two recovery-contract corrections below. The direction is faithful and suitable for a small team; it is not yet an implementation-ready safety claim. Keep A01–A08 as real qualification/release gates. This recommendation does not authorize a build or reopen OD01–OD09.

The strongest choices are the modular monolith, one relational transaction system, separate web/worker processes, managed authentication and no additional cache/warehouse/service fleet. The proposal preserves business and courier truth, case-only completion, cardinality, Admin authority, provenance and nonadditive product populations. Tenant keys, composite relationships, RLS and checked request/job context form useful complementary defenses. It also correctly acknowledges that platform pool separation cannot contain complete process compromise.

External-action handling is unusually careful: immutable intent, durable objective reservation, one precommitted dispatch, independent effects/results, unknown blocking both connected and manual execution, and no automatic compensation. Public provider contracts are distinguished from account qualification. These safeguards should survive simplification.

**Assumptions:** the team can review TypeScript and explicit PostgreSQL transactions/grants; the named pilot accounts can demonstrate a useful supported round trip and reliable result identification; owners approve source-authority, analytics, privacy and commercial-continuity policies; someone owns unknown-result investigation and recovery. None of those operational capabilities is verified by architecture documentation alone.

1. **P1 — The recovery journal cannot yet reconstruct every lost reservation.** In [13-deployment-and-environments.md](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/13-deployment-and-environments.md), “Protecting external effects across backup rollback,” markers contain local objective/intent/effect IDs, hashes and connection/correlation references. If an objective was created after the restored backup, its local IDs and hash do not identify the original business subject, effect kind or reservation scope. Consequently “rebuild missing objective reservations” is an aspiration rather than a complete recovery contract. A conservative global block prevents immediate duplicates but offers no defined route to safely resume unrelated work. Specify a minimal protected recovery descriptor: verified source namespace/native subject references, effect kind, stable business reservation scope, dependencies and descriptor version, without customer payloads. Define enumeration completeness and reconstruction/hold rules. Extend [14-testing-strategy.md](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/14-testing-strategy.md) with a restore preceding objective creation, including an unresolved lost-response effect. Do not turn the journal into full event sourcing.

2. **P1 — Completed privacy redactions may be forgotten by rollback.** [11-audit-provenance-and-privacy.md](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/11-audit-provenance-and-privacy.md), “Provider data requests and redaction,” and [13](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/13-deployment-and-environments.md), “Backup and restore,” require deletion-manifest replay before reopening. They do not specify where manifests survive independently of the restored database. A deletion completed after the restore point can otherwise disappear with its manifest, resurrecting data despite a successful replay of the older manifests. 3. **P2 — Revenue attribution needs a clearer contract than spend allocation.** [09-analytics-architecture.md](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/09-analytics-architecture.md), “Canonical ROAS,” requires attributed commerce revenue, while [04](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/04-data-architecture.md) explicitly models spend mappings/allocations and revenue adjustments. An item-to-product link plus ad-to-product spend mapping does not by itself establish revenue eligibility for K07. Include an analytical fixture with attributed and unattributed sales of the same SKU; absent an approved rule, qualify/unavailable the numerator rather than silently using all sales. OP09 chooses the rule; engineering must not infer it.

4. **P2 — Bound the physical implementation of the provenance/module design.** [02](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/02-architecture-style-and-modules.md) has eleven modules; [04](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/04-data-architecture.md)/[11](D:/Desktop/Opshub/docs/architecture/11-audit-provenance-and-privacy.md) combine typed state, assertions, interpretations, revisions and projections. These meanings are justified, but implementing every logical record as a separate repository/framework would overwhelm the team and multiply coordination mistakes. Make the stated permission to combine closely related physical records concrete: fixed business-specific interpretation contracts, one checked unit-of-work mechanism, one outbox protocol, bounded retained generations and explicit ownership of each current value. Preserve safety-critical identities and evidence; prohibit a generic policy/EAV/CQRS engine. The selected stack is reasonable, but toolchain/grant compatibility and operating costs remain qualifications, not demonstrated advantages.

The strongest objection to approval is that a broad integrated MVP plus this safety/provenance burden may exceed the team's capacity, while unresolved provider outcomes can immobilize daily work. My response is conditional: architecture approval is sensible, but pilot release must fail if controlled qualification cannot demonstrate recoverable essential actions and staffed exception handling. Documentation quality cannot substitute for those proofs.
